BMI Calculator

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m).
📌 Reference / interpretationAdult BMI: <18.5 underweight • 18.5–<25 healthy weight • 25–<30 overweight • ≥30 obesity. Obesity class 1: 30–<35; class 2: 35–<40; class 3: ≥40. BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis.

BSA Calculator

Mosteller: BSA = √[(height × weight) ÷ 3600].
📌 ReferenceAdult BSA varies with body size; ~1.7 m² is a rough typical adult value. There is no single universal “normal” BSA range—use the calculated value for context and dosing protocols.

Ideal Body Weight

Devine: male 50 kg + 2.3 kg/inch over 5 ft; female 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg/inch over 5 ft.
📌 ReferenceIBW is formula-derived, not a normal-weight reference range. Devine formula: male 50 kg + 2.3 kg/in above 5 ft; female 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg/in above 5 ft.

Adjusted Body Weight

Common estimate: AdjBW = IBW + 0.4 × (ABW − IBW).
📌 ReferenceAdjusted body weight is an estimate used in selected clinical/drug-dosing contexts. A common formula is IBW + 0.4 × (actual weight − IBW); follow the drug-specific protocol when applicable.

Glasgow Coma Scale

📌 Reference / interpretationTotal GCS ranges 3–15. 15 = fully alert/oriented response on the scale; lower scores indicate greater impairment. ≤8 is commonly treated as severe impairment and requires urgent clinical assessment. Record E/V/M components separately when possible.

APGAR Score

📌 Reference / interpretationEach component scores 0–2; total 0–10. At 5 minutes: 7–10 reassuring • 4–6 moderately abnormal • 0–3 low. APGAR describes newborn condition and response to resuscitation; it should not be used alone to diagnose asphyxia or predict individual neurologic outcome.

qSOFA

qSOFA is a bedside risk-stratification score and is not, by itself, a diagnostic test for sepsis.
📌 Reference / interpretationqSOFA = 0–3. One point each for RR ≥22/min, altered mentation, SBP ≤100 mmHg. ≥2 indicates higher risk of poor outcomes in suspected infection and should prompt further assessment; qSOFA is not a stand-alone sepsis diagnosis.

Anion Gap

AG = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻), excluding potassium.
📌 ReferenceTypical anion gap without potassium is roughly 8–12 mEq/L, but the reference interval is laboratory-dependent. Interpret with albumin, acid-base status and local laboratory range.

Corrected Calcium

Common estimate: corrected Ca = measured Ca + 0.8 × (4.0 − albumin).
Albumin-based correction is an estimate; ionized calcium may be more clinically informative in appropriate settings.
📌 ReferenceTotal serum calcium is commonly around 8.5–10.5 mg/dL in adults, but ranges vary by laboratory. Albumin-corrected calcium is an estimate; ionized calcium is preferable when clinically indicated.

Creatinine Clearance

Cockcroft–Gault estimate: [(140 − age) × weight] ÷ (72 × SCr); multiply by 0.85 for female sex.
Drug dosing decisions require the appropriate clinical method, patient context and current prescribing guidance.
📌 Reference / interpretationCreatinine clearance is an estimate rather than a fixed “normal” value. In healthy young adults it is often roughly around 90–120 mL/min and generally declines with age. For drug dosing, use the method specified by the drug reference/protocol.

eGFR — CKD-EPI 2021

CKD-EPI 2021 creatinine equation without race: eGFR = 142 × min(SCr/k,1)^α × max(SCr/k,1)^−1.200 × 0.9938^Age × 1.012 if female; k=0.9 male/0.7 female, α=−0.302 male/−0.241 female.
Use appropriate laboratory/clinical guidance; eGFR has limitations in several clinical situations.
📌 Reference / KDIGO categoriesG1 ≥90 • G2 60–89 • G3a 45–59 • G3b 30–44 • G4 15–29 • G5 <15 mL/min/1.73 m². G1/G2 alone do not establish CKD without other evidence of kidney damage.

Corrected Sodium

Common estimate: corrected Na ≈ measured Na + 1.6 × [(glucose − 100) ÷ 100].
Different correction factors are used in clinical references; interpret in context.
📌 ReferenceTypical adult serum sodium is about 135–145 mEq/L. Corrected sodium is an estimate used in hyperglycemia; correction factors vary, so interpret alongside the clinical picture and local guidance.

Calculated Serum Osmolality

Calculated osmolality ≈ 2×Na + glucose/18 + BUN/2.8.
📌 ReferenceTypical measured serum osmolality is approximately 275–295 mOsm/kg. Calculated osmolality is an estimate and may differ from measured osmolality.

Delta Ratio

Delta ratio = (AG − 12) ÷ (24 − HCO₃⁻). Interpretation requires clinical context.
📌 Reference / interpretationDelta ratio = (AG−12)/(24−HCO₃). A value around 1–2 is commonly compatible with a predominant high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis; <1 suggests an additional normal-gap acidosis, while >2 may suggest a concurrent metabolic alkalosis or chronic respiratory acidosis. Use clinical context.

Wells Criteria for Pulmonary Embolism

📌 Reference / interpretation≤4 = PE unlikely; >4 = PE likely (two-tier model). This score estimates pre-test probability; it does not diagnose PE.

CURB-65

📌 Reference / interpretation0–1: lower risk • 2: moderate risk • ≥3: high risk. The score supports severity/disposition assessment and does not replace clinical judgment.

CHA₂DS₂-VASc

📌 ReferenceMaximum score 9. Components: CHF 1, HTN 1, age ≥75 2, diabetes 1, stroke/TIA/TE 2, vascular disease 1, age 65–74 1, female sex 1. Anticoagulation decisions should follow current AF guidance and patient-specific risk.

HAS-BLED

📌 Reference / interpretationScore range 0–9. ≥3 indicates increased bleeding risk and should prompt review of modifiable risk factors; it is not by itself a reason to withhold anticoagulation.

Child-Pugh Score

📌 Reference / interpretationTypical non-cholestatic scoring: bilirubin <2 / 2–3 / >3 mg/dL = 1/2/3; albumin >3.5 / 2.8–3.5 / <2.8 g/dL = 1/2/3; INR <1.7 / 1.7–2.3 / >2.3 = 1/2/3. Class A 5–6 • B 7–9 • C 10–15. Ascites and encephalopathy are clinically graded.

MELD — Basic Reference Calculator

This version uses the classic MELD equation: 3.78×ln(bilirubin) + 11.2×ln(INR) + 9.57×ln(creatinine) + 6.43, with values bounded at 1–40 and a minimum score of 6.
📌 ReferenceMELD has multiple modern versions (including MELD-3.0 and MELD-Na). This basic tool is for educational use; transplant allocation should use the current system and official calculator.

Bishop Score

📌 Reference / interpretationClassic Bishop score range 0–13. ≥8 is generally considered a favorable cervix; lower scores indicate an unfavorable cervix. Exact induction protocols vary.

EDD Calculator

Naegele's rule: LMP + 280 days (40 weeks), assuming a reliable LMP and typical cycle length.
📌 ReferenceStandard Naegele calculation uses 280 days (40 weeks) from the first day of LMP, assuming a reliable LMP and typical cycle length. Dating may be revised using ultrasound according to clinical guidance.

Gestational Age

Gestational age is calculated from the first day of the LMP.
📌 ReferenceGestational age is conventionally counted from the first day of the LMP. A term pregnancy is generally 37+0 to 41+6 weeks; exact dating should follow obstetric dating guidance.

Mean Arterial Pressure

Approximate MAP = DBP + ⅓(SBP − DBP).
📌 ReferenceMAP varies with age and clinical state. A commonly used bedside target in critically ill adults is ≥65 mmHg in appropriate settings; this is a treatment target, not a universal normal range.

Pulse Pressure

Pulse pressure = SBP − DBP.
📌 ReferencePulse pressure = SBP−DBP. A typical resting adult value is around 40 mmHg, but it varies with age, stroke volume and vascular stiffness. Interpret with the blood pressure and clinical context.

IV Drip Rate

mL/hr = volume ÷ hours. drops/min = (volume × drop factor) ÷ time in minutes.
📌 ReferenceThere is no single normal drip rate. Use the prescribed volume, duration and the infusion set’s drop factor. Common gravity sets may be 10, 15, 20 or 60 drops/mL—verify the actual set.

Infusion Rate

📌 ReferenceThere is no universal normal infusion rate. Calculate from the prescribed volume and duration, then verify the medication/fluid-specific rate and pump limits.

Weight-based Dose

This performs arithmetic only. It does not determine whether a medication, dose, frequency, route or maximum dose is appropriate.
📌 ReferenceThere is no universal normal dose. This tool only multiplies mg/kg × weight. Always verify drug, indication, age, renal/hepatic function, route, frequency, maximum dose and local protocol.
